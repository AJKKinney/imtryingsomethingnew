## 1. The Early Access block — ~250 words, and it blocks the page

Valve requires six answers and renders them **above** the description. A hundred and
thirty-seven passes never mentioned it, in a plan whose entire launch is Early Access and
whose first draft named the price. It is on the critical path at **phase 3b**, not phase 6.
Every answer is already determined:

| Valve asks | The answer |
|---|---|
| Why Early Access? | The acceptance bands are calibrated on bots and one playtester. The game needs a population to finish balancing — which is the honest reason and also the true one |
| How long? | Three content drops and 1.0 at a 4–6 week cadence: **18–28 weeks.** A range, not a date |
| How will the full version differ? | The Ring core, the daily and its leaderboard, the replay viewer, duty ratings 4–10, an anomaly set, three anomalies, achievements 20 → 30, a fourth core, and the Substrate biome |
| Current state? | Stated as capability: three cores, fourteen drafted components, six enemies, six encounters, duty ratings −3…+3, the Hall, the graveyard, the daily seed, WORKSHOP |
| Will the price change? | **$9.99 → $12.99 at 1.0.** Said plainly, which Valve's guidance prefers over silence |
| Community involvement? | The daily, the graveyard, the Discord and the forum, and opt-out telemetry with its own document |

## 2. The claim, in one sentence

> **Spatial routing where the cost of a placement is *thermal* and varies with how hard the
> fight is going** — power wants compact, heat wants spread, and the heat depends on the war
> rather than the layout.

No neighbour combines those. Nova Drift has the pressure without the space; Backpack Hero
the space without the live cost; Opus Magnum the space without the pressure.

**And the 300-character short description carries one more clause: *while you are being
chased*.** §123.1 measured the gap this closes — the game is a 7 on the head/hands scale by
its own tags, a 5.5 by outcome, and **85% of its minutes feel like a 2.3**. That is not a
balance error, since §68.2 chose the positioning and §87.1 validated the outcome share, but
the promise had never been stated *in time*, and a mis-set expectation is the cheapest
negative review there is.

## 3. The hook, shown

The peak-moment capsule, then a loop of the trailer's 0:10–0:16 shot: `TAB`, the world
drops to 20% time, a component is picked up and re-placed, and the region-heat contour moves
with it. **The product, shown inside the pressure rather than beside it.**

## 4. Three blocks of what you do

*Route the power · court the overclock · answer the meltdown.* One animated capture each,
rendered from the same seeds as the trailer, so they cannot depict a version of the game
that no longer exists.

## 5. What makes it different

> **Your machine can destroy itself.**

§87.4 is why this is the line and greed is not: against the real neighbours — Nova Drift,
Backpack Hero, Opus Magnum, **Balatro** — greed is taken. Balatro is entirely about greed.
None of the four can melt down.

## 6. The resonance — the paragraph people repeat

Not the mechanic. *You find the wreck of your own machine from last night, by name, and take
a part off it.* Then: *the wrecks in your field are other people's, at your own skill, in
colours you did not choose* — and *the final boss is composited from them, with your own
machines visible in the hull.*

§55.5's rule: **lead with what people repeat, not with what the game does.**

## 7. The reason to return

The daily — one seed, one scored attempt, the same run for everyone on every platform — and
**PAR**, three bot policies run locally on today's seed, bit-identical on every machine
because the simulation is deterministic. That is what gives a player with no Steam, no
friends and no network an opponent their own size.

## 8. Venue and access

Deck Verified. Fourteen duty rungs, in **both** directions. Board time-scale down to
*paused*. Colourblind palettes day one, mandatory reduce-flashing, type legible at 1280×800
and at ten feet. **Sound-off complete.**

## 9. What it is not

One honest line: **no manual aim, no co-op, no revives.**

§42.2's cheapest defence against a negative review is telling the wrong buyer before they
buy — which is §62.3's own argument for the free web build, applied to the description.

## The structural note (§133.5)

**MELTLINE is a bullet heaven that slows down so you can think, and then does not let you
stop.** That was carried as a *risk* for a hundred and twenty sections — the considered
interaction inside a reflex game — and it is the most distinctive thing about the game.
**It is the hook, not the apology.**

## Pricing is a schedule, not a number (§144.6)

A price stated as a scalar reads exactly like a fact. Every revenue figure in this project
is a **band over this block**.

- **Launch week −12%.** Then **nothing** for the rest of month one: §42.2's review window is
  decided by buyers who paid, and discounting into it undercuts them.
- **Nothing at Next Fest** — the demo is the offer, and the game is unreleased.
- **−15 to −20%** at seasonal sales during Early Access. A sale is a free visibility event
  and §21's drops are the reason to be seen.
- **At 1.0 the price rises to $12.99 *before* the ladder deepens.** Sequencing is worth real
  money: −33% off $12.99 is $8.70 against −20% off $9.99 at $7.99.
- **−25 to −33%** in year one; **−40 to −50%** in year two.
- **Never below −50% before year two, and never a permanent list-price cut.** A permanent
  cut is unrecoverable and tells every wishlister to wait; a deep sale is an event.

**Regional pricing is accepted at Valve's suggested values — blended ×0.78** — because the
objective is units, and the four most deeply adjusted regions are the four languages the UI
ships in at Early Access.

**Checked and kept:** at ~33 hours to the terminal goal, $9.99 is **$0.30 an hour** and
$12.99 is **$0.39** — under the rough indie norm, so the game is *under*priced on content.
It stays there because $9.99 is where discovery lives and §42.2's flywheel wants the
conversion.

## The calendar — three dated objects this plan is planned backwards from (§146.6)

Each carries a `verified-against` date, and each is a claim about the world rather than a
decision made here.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Next Fest edition** | Chosen and **registered at the end of phase 4**, targeting the edition after phase 5's expected completion. It is once per game, unrepeatable, and §62.5's target scenario names a Gold fest among its three components — so missing it is worth **7,100 copies and $31,000–35,400**. **If phase 5 slips past it, the fest is taken anyway with the build that exists**, because phase 5 is growth machinery a fest visitor never sees. Registration not completed by the end of phase 5 is a stop-condition-class event, not a slip |
| **The release date** | A required Steamworks field with no owner until §146.5. Set with the fest registration. **2–8 weeks after the fest; never within two weeks of or during a seasonal sale; never in the second half of December; preferably 2–4 weeks after a sale closes; Tuesday to Thursday** |
| **The sale windows** | Content drops are scheduled **to the calendar**, not to a metronome. A drop, a sale and a visibility round arriving together is one event with three multipliers; a 5-to-6.5-week metronome aligns with a quarterly sale about 0.9 times out of three. The 4–6 week cadence becomes a floor: **never longer than eight weeks** |

**Every dated entry also names the build it requires and the phase that produces it**
(§148.6). A Next Fest listing is a **demo app on the Steam store page**, not a web URL — the
safety net was attached to the wrong artifact once, which is why the Electron shell moved to
phase 4.
